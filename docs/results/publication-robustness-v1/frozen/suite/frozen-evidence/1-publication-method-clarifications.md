# Dated PI-review corrections and clarifications

October 23, 2026. These corrections supplement the historical frozen protocols; original predictions, scores, declaration files and their hashes remain unchanged.

1. The original five primary seeds vary the policy's pilot identity order and tie choices on one fixed episode and fixed per-condition cell order. They do **not** vary neural initialization or resample cells. All optional neural fits use model seed 20261022. Direct-query Task C separately varies cell order. The frozen external protocol's phrase "acquisition/initialization variability" was too broad.
2. The original real-data rank-four coordinates plus intercept are already fully supported at the first reported budget after the six-identity pilot. Real results primarily test conditional precision/shrinkage allocation, not an identification transition. The pilot costs 88/128=68.75% of the first ceiling. Identification failures are separately constructed known-truth simulation diagnostics. A declared new split/rank/budget robustness study addresses this limitation without relabeling it as confirmatory.
3. The reconstructed transfer gate had a diagnostic epsilon bug: `1-route_mse/(base_mse+1e-12)` gives positive reliability for equal errors when base error is positive. Among 720 original checkpoints, 20 zero-alpha routes were incorrectly marked accepted. Saved reconstruction/base prediction arrays are identical at these checkpoints; other 700 have strictly positive fitted calibration gain. The original metrics remain historical. Version 0.2.0 requires positive interpolation and positive actual gain; an explicit audit will retain its evidence.
4. The reconstruction reuses paid validation labels for early stopping, ridge selection and interpolation. Reported gain is an in-sample calibration-fit statistic, not independent reliability or a formal harm test. Even correct routing flags do not validate transfer. The comparator is an explicitly qualified algorithm reconstruction, not reproduction or refutation of PerturbMap's published performance.
5. The fixed RBF discrepancy-variance term does not learn realized structural bias and is not the selected ridge predictor's risk. The paper evaluates this specified augmentation, not bias-aware acquisition generally. Ideal Gaussian widths centered on ridge predictions remain failure diagnostics, not an uncertainty estimator with established coverage after adaptive/model/source error.

The previous implementation goal's audit is a historical snapshot. It does not imply that a formal manuscript, these PI-requested analyses, or publication readiness already existed at that milestone.
